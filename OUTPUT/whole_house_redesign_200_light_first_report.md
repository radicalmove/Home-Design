# 200 Light-First Whole-House Redesign Scenarios Report

Date: 2026-06-04
Project: Current House
Purpose: Restart the 200-design exercise with daylight and daily-use fit as the primary design driver.

## Why This Restart Was Needed

The previous 200-scenario report treated daylight as a supporting issue. That was wrong for this house. The strongest light is not simply a nice bonus; it is one of the main design constraints. The better question is not "which rooms exist now?" but "where does the useful light land, and are people actually spending time there when it is good?"

The revised brief also includes new household-specific facts:

- The real arrival route is from the rear/garage/back side, not the sunroom/front side.
- The sunroom is not automatically useful as an entry or retreat because it has thermal and privacy problems unless upgraded with heating/cooling, curtains/blinds, ventilation, and better control.
- The laundry and toilet work acceptably now, but the laundry/entrance side receives comparatively useful light. It is therefore reasonable to consider moving laundry/WC functions if that unlocks a more valuable room use.
- A second WC may be better solved by adding a toilet into a bathroom redesign, rather than assuming the current laundry/toilet zone must stay.
- The kitchen has recently been renovated, so kitchen changes are possible but should be treated as unlikely unless they unlock a major whole-house benefit.

## Light And Use Assumptions

The daylight model and photos support this practical reading:

- Sunroom: strongest daylight asset, but unreliable comfort unless thermally controlled.
- Kitchen / Dining: useful midday and seasonal light, narrow but connected to deck.
- Lounge: important shared room, but mostly borrowed/medium light and has too many routes.
- Bedroom 2: weaker afternoon/winter light and needs teen sleep/study/privacy function.
- Office: useful as quiet work space but not a strong all-day light room.
- Entrance / laundry side: better morning to early-afternoon light than its current low-use service role deserves.
- Toilet: low light and short-stay use, so it can move or be absorbed elsewhere if plumbing and clearances allow.
- Deck: potentially high lifestyle value but needs shade, wind control, and better back-entry logic.

## New Scoring Method

Each scenario is judged by:

1. Daylight-use match: does useful light move toward dining, sitting, work, study, or family life?
2. Major-problem solving: does it address lounge pressure, arrival, deck usability, sunroom comfort, teen function, and WC practicality?
3. Cost and disruption: does the gain justify wet-area, structural, or exterior work?
4. Kitchen preservation: does it avoid undoing a recent renovation unless clearly worthwhile?
5. Staging quality: can the idea be tested or built in sensible phases?

## Revised Top 10 After Light-First Review

### 1. Bright-End Living Reallocation Masterplan

Best source scenarios: 017, 029, 033, 140, 181, 200.

What changes: Move laundry and current WC functions out of the bright laundry/toilet/entrance corner. Add WC to the bathroom or a darker compact powder room. Put laundry into a well-ventilated cupboard, garage-adjacent utility, or service-core position. Reuse the current laundry/toilet/entrance light as a small sunny sitting, study, breakfast, or family day room connected to the deck/back entry. Keep the renovated kitchen. Calm the lounge as an evening/media room and improve Bedroom 2 with storage/privacy/task lighting.

Why it ranks first: It directly fixes the daylight-use mismatch rather than decorating around it. It also solves or improves back arrival, one-toilet pressure, lounge pressure, teen study options, and deck relationship.

Builder refinement: This must start with plumbing and floor-structure feasibility. The design is only worth doing if the new laundry and WC locations are clean, ventilated, legal, and not awkward.

Ballpark: NZD 95k to 230k depending on plumbing routes, bathroom scope, joinery, and whether deck work is included.

### 2. Covered Deck + Back Entry Social Core

Best source scenarios: 041, 042, 046, 053, 182, 193.

What changes: Treat the back/garage/deck side as the real arrival. Add a properly designed deck roof/louvre/pergola with wind and sun control, better steps, lighting, power, and a clearer path from cars/garage. Keep the kitchen intact and let the deck become the daytime/social extension of kitchen/dining. Laundry/WC relocation is optional but planned for.

Why it ranks high: It corrects the arrival assumption and makes an existing asset useful. It is less disruptive than moving wet areas first, and it can be staged.

Builder refinement: Do not build a dark roof that steals light from kitchen/dining/entrance. The shade design needs winter-sun and summer-glare thinking, not just a generic cover.

Ballpark: NZD 55k to 180k depending on cover, structure, wind screens, lighting, and entry work.

### 3. Bathroom WC + Compact Laundry + Bright Room

Best source scenarios: 010, 025, 029, 034, 121, 183.

What changes: Redesign the bathroom to include a WC if clearances allow. Move laundry to a compact cupboard or service-core location. Reclaim the current laundry/toilet area as a bright daily-use room or back-entry sitting/study zone. Kitchen stays. Deck and lounge improvements are paired but can be staged.

Why it ranks high: It solves the toilet bottleneck and the light mismatch together. It is more coherent than simply adding another WC while leaving the brightest service room unchanged.

Builder refinement: The bathroom must not become cramped just to fit a WC. If fixture clearances are poor, a separate compact powder room may be better.

Ballpark: NZD 80k to 200k.

### 4. Whole-House Medium-Budget Hybrid

Best source scenarios: 030, 045, 048, 125, 188, 198.

What changes: Add bathroom WC if feasible, compact or partially reorient laundry, improve deck shade/back entry, calm lounge with lighting/glazed control, add sunroom curtains/thermal control, and improve Bedroom 2. This may not fully move the laundry, but it reduces the waste of the bright service zone.

Why it ranks high: It is the best practical compromise if a full wet-area relocation is too expensive.

Builder refinement: Decide what not to do. A medium-budget hybrid can become expensive if it touches every room without fully solving any one issue.

Ballpark: NZD 70k to 165k.

### 5. Sunroom Thermal Day Room + Lounge Borrowed-Light Reset

Best source scenarios: 006, 061, 067, 084, 098, 184.

What changes: Upgrade sunroom comfort enough that it becomes a real day room rather than a seasonal storage/entry oddity. Add heat/cooling, blinds/curtains, privacy, ventilation, and better door/glazing performance. Lounge becomes calmer and benefits from controlled borrowed light. Deck/back entry still gets targeted work.

Why it ranks high: It uses the strongest daylight asset without pretending the sunroom is already comfortable.

Builder refinement: Inspect the sunroom shell first. If it is a poor structure, avoid spending on finishes before deciding repair versus rebuild.

Ballpark: NZD 45k to 180k.

### 6. Covered Deck Dining Expansion While Preserving Kitchen

Best source scenarios: 043, 049, 052, 054, 152, 157.

What changes: Preserve the renovated kitchen, keep indoor dining compact, and make the covered deck the flexible dining/social expansion. Improve back entry from garage, weather protection, and lighting. Lounge stays evening/media. Sunroom is seasonal or lightly upgraded.

Why it ranks high: It respects the kitchen constraint and improves the most obvious indoor-outdoor living problem.

Builder refinement: The deck roof must be designed around light. Poor cover could make the kitchen/dining/entrance darker and create a new problem.

Ballpark: NZD 45k to 150k.

### 7. Lounge Evening Room + New Bright Day Room

Best source scenarios: 086, 096, 097, 120, 140, 199.

What changes: Stop asking the lounge to be the bright daytime room. Make it a comfortable evening/media/fireplace room. Create the daytime room elsewhere: either reclaimed laundry/entrance, upgraded sunroom, or covered deck. Bedroom 2 and office remain quieter private rooms.

Why it ranks high: It accepts the lounge's limitations instead of forcing it to be something it is not.

Builder refinement: This is only strong if the replacement day room is actually comfortable in temperature, privacy, and circulation.

Ballpark: NZD 40k to 190k depending on which day room is created.

### 8. Back Entry + Bright Mudroom/Sitting Hybrid

Best source scenarios: 018, 022, 023, 037, 133, 171.

What changes: Keep some service practicality near the back entry, but make the bright zone do more than laundry. Add hidden storage, a sunny bench/window seat, better arrival sequence from garage, improved deck entry, and possibly move WC to bathroom.

Builder refinement: The feasibility work must be specific: underfloor access, waste gradients, ventilation routes, structural walls, deck roof support, and consent implications.

Ballpark: NZD 5k to 20k for investigation/design/testing; then NZD 60k to 220k depending on selected build path.

## Designs Demoted After User Feedback

- Dual sunroom/front-entry plans: demoted because actual arrival is from the rear/garage/back side.
- Laundry-only refit: demoted because the laundry works acceptably and the bigger issue is whether it occupies too much useful light.
- Quiet household plan relying on sunroom: demoted unless sunroom temperature/privacy is solved.
- Deck-only plans: still useful, but lower if they fail to reclaim or compensate for the bright service-end mismatch.
- Bathroom-only WC plans: useful, but weaker than plans that add WC and reclaim the bright laundry/toilet zone.

## Best Current Recommendation

The best direction is not just "cover the deck" or "upgrade the sunroom". The strongest design direction is a light-first whole-house reallocation:

1. Preserve the renovated kitchen unless a designer proves a major benefit.
2. Treat the back/garage/deck side as the true arrival.
3. Add a proper shade/wind/weather solution to the deck without killing indoor light.
4. Investigate moving laundry and WC functions out of the bright service end.
5. Add a WC to the bathroom or a darker compact powder room if clearances allow.
6. Reuse the current laundry/toilet/entrance daylight as a room people actually occupy.
7. Make the lounge a calm evening/media room rather than forcing it to be the bright daytime room.
8. Treat the sunroom as a daylight asset only if thermal/privacy controls are included.
9. Support Bedroom 2 with privacy, storage, task lighting, and access to better shared daylight.

If the wet-area move proves too expensive or awkward, the fallback should be the Covered Deck + Back Entry Social Core, paired with bathroom WC feasibility and a lighter-touch service-zone improvement.
