# Future House Design Options Report

Date: 2026-06-03
Project: Current House
Purpose: Generate 50 redesign options, critique them from a practical builder/interior designer perspective, and shortlist the best 5 for this household.

## Important Assumptions

This is a concept report, not a construction drawing, engineering assessment, builder quote, or consent review. Costs are deliberately broad 2026 NZD ballparks and should be checked with local trades, a designer, and council/consent advice before decisions.

The kitchen has recently been renovated, so the default assumption is to preserve it. Kitchen changes are not impossible, but they should have a much higher threshold: only consider them if they unlock a major improvement that cannot be achieved through entry, lounge, sunroom, deck, bedroom, or laundry changes. Shortlisted options therefore avoid moving the main kitchen cabinet runs, sink, cooktop, fridge, pantry, dishwasher, or new kitchen finishes unless explicitly marked as a higher-cost exception.

The household use case is two adults in the master bedroom and a teenage girl in Bedroom 2. The design priorities are daily comfort, privacy, movement flow, useful light, entertaining, quiet retreat areas, and cost practicality.

## Current Problems To Solve

The house is workable, but the pressure points are clear:

- The lounge is asked to be a media room, sitting room, route to the sunroom, route to kitchen/dining, and route from the hallway. That makes it harder to feel calm.
- The lounge does not receive the best direct light. It borrows light from the sunroom and small windows, so clutter, dark furnishings, and blocked openings matter.
- The sunroom is the strongest daylight asset but is seasonally unstable: too hot in summer, too cold in winter, and awkward as the visitor entry sequence.
- The deck is a genuine lifestyle asset but is exposed to sun and wind. The umbrella is not a robust solution.
- The kitchen/dining strip works, but it is narrow and should not be overloaded with extra storage or heavy furniture.
- The entrance/laundry side gets useful light, but it is a transition/service zone rather than a room people linger in.
- Bedroom 2 is long and narrow. It can work for a teenager, but it needs a clear path, storage discipline, privacy, and task lighting.
- There is only one toilet, accessed through the laundry. This is a real family/guest bottleneck.
- The front door is not obvious. The original front door is awkwardly inside the sunroom; the practical daily entry is deck/entrance side.

## Cost Bands Used

These are planning bands only:

- Furniture, lighting, curtains, paint, and joinery refresh: $2k to $25k
- Internal doors, partial walls, lining, flooring repairs, small framing changes: $8k to $45k
- Deck shade, pergola, roof, screens, drainage, lighting: $15k to $75k
- Wet-area rework or second WC: $30k to $95k
- Sunroom thermal upgrade: $20k to $80k
- Sunroom rebuild or small extension: $80k to $220k
- Larger extension: $180k plus, and likely less aligned with the brief

For any structural wall removal, exterior envelope change, wet-area relocation, new roof, deck roof, or extension, assume design/engineering/consent may be needed.

## 50 Redesign Options With Critique

### 1. Furniture-only lounge simplification

Remove or relocate non-essential lounge furniture, keep one strong sofa/TV/fireplace relationship, move some bookcases or music/storage pieces out of circulation, and protect the route to the sunroom.

Builder/interior critique: Very low risk and high value. It will not solve the architectural light/entry issues, but it should immediately make the lounge feel calmer. Best first move before spending heavily.

Indicative cost: $0 to $5k.

### 2. Lounge storage wall around fireplace and TV

Create a built-in media/storage wall around the fireplace and TV, with shallow closed storage, cable management, and lighter finishes.

Builder/interior critique: Good for reducing visual clutter. Must avoid making the already darker lounge feel heavier. A pale, shallow, integrated solution would be better than more freestanding furniture.

Indicative cost: $6k to $25k.

### 3. Add glazed internal doors between lounge and kitchen/dining opening

Fit glazed French doors, sliding doors, or a wide glazed pocket/stacker system in the 1.3m lounge-to-kitchen/dining opening.

Builder/interior critique: Useful for acoustic separation and making the lounge feel like a room rather than a corridor. Because the kitchen is recently renovated, the door set should fit the existing opening without altering kitchen cabinetry.

Indicative cost: $8k to $25k.

### 4. Partially close the lounge-to-kitchen/dining opening

Reduce the opening with a partial nib, shelving return, or framed wall section so the lounge has a more defined wall edge.

Builder/interior critique: Could improve lounge calm but risks making the kitchen/dining strip feel more enclosed. It also may worsen the connection to dining and borrowed light. Only worth testing in the app first.

Indicative cost: $8k to $30k.

### 5. Widen the lounge-to-kitchen/dining opening

Open the lounge further to the dining end while retaining the kitchen cabinetry.

Builder/interior critique: Might improve social connection and light, but it makes the lounge even more of a through-space. Structural checks likely. Not a top choice because the problem is too many openings, not too few.

Indicative cost: $15k to $55k.

### 6. Close the hallway-to-lounge door and access lounge only via kitchen/dining

Remove or close the hallway lounge doorway so the lounge has one less circulation path.

Builder/interior critique: Spatially tempting, but it may make daily movement worse and force too much traffic through kitchen/dining. It could work only if the kitchen/dining entry remains clear and the hallway still feels logical.

Indicative cost: $6k to $20k.

### 7. Move the hallway-to-lounge door slightly or replace it with a cavity slider

Keep the connection but make the doorway less disruptive to lounge furniture placement.

Builder/interior critique: Moderate value. A cavity slider can reduce door-swing conflict, but it will not solve the lounge being a junction. Worth considering if other lounge works are happening.

Indicative cost: $8k to $25k.

### 8. Create a defined reading/music nook in the sunroom

Treat the sunroom as a deliberate light-rich retreat with reading chair, music/piano/play storage, plants, and low closed storage.

Builder/interior critique: Strong lifestyle value at low cost. It uses the best light in the house and relieves the lounge. It needs thermal and clutter control or it becomes seasonal storage again.

Indicative cost: $2k to $15k.

### 9. Sunroom thermal upgrade without changing footprint

Improve blinds, seals, insulation where feasible, heating/cooling, ventilation, and summer shade to make the sunroom usable more often.

Builder/interior critique: Very sensible if the sunroom is to remain. It addresses a major weakness without the cost of demolition/rebuild. Check condensation, glazing quality, and whether the structure is worth investing in.

Indicative cost: $15k to $60k.

### 10. Rebuild the sunroom as an insulated all-season room

Replace the current sunroom envelope with a properly insulated, double-glazed, code-compliant room while keeping the broad footprint and lounge connection.

Builder/interior critique: Potentially transformational, but much more expensive. It would turn the best light zone into real living space, but it may trigger consent and exterior envelope complexity.

Indicative cost: $90k to $220k.

### 11. Demolish the sunroom and restore a simpler exterior/front porch

Remove the sunroom, create a smaller covered entry/porch, and give the lounge more direct exterior outlook.

Builder/interior critique: This clarifies the front and may remove a poor thermal appendage, but it loses the best daylight room and useful flexible space. I would not shortlist it unless the sunroom structure is poor.

Indicative cost: $40k to $120k.

### 12. Convert the sunroom into a true front entry and sitting porch

Keep the sunroom but reorganise it so visitors enter a clear, attractive glazed foyer with seating, plants, storage, and a direct visual route into the house.

Builder/interior critique: Strong response to the unclear front door. It retains the daylight benefit and improves arrival, but must avoid making the sunroom feel like a cluttered mudroom.

Indicative cost: $10k to $45k.

### 13. Add an external canopy and better path sequence to the sunroom doors

Improve the approach to the angled sunroom doors with path lighting, step correction, a canopy, door hardware, house number, and planting cues.

Builder/interior critique: Good front-door clarity for modest cost. It does not solve internal sunroom thermal comfort, but it makes visitor arrival much more intuitive.

Indicative cost: $8k to $35k.

### 14. Move the formal front door identity to the deck/entrance side

Accept the deck-side entrance as the real front door, then add a covered landing, lighting, address marker, door colour, and a direct path cue.

Builder/interior critique: Very practical. It follows how the house appears to work now. The risk is that visitors may still approach the old front/sunroom side unless the site/path cues are changed clearly.

Indicative cost: $8k to $40k.

### 15. Build a small enclosed deck-side entry porch

Add a compact weather-protected porch or vestibule outside the entrance slider, with shoe/coat handling inside or immediately adjacent.

Builder/interior critique: Good daily-life improvement. It can also reduce heat loss and make the deck-side entry feel intentional. Avoid crowding the existing deck seating zone.

Indicative cost: $25k to $80k.

### 16. Turn the entrance/laundry zone into a proper mudroom-laundry

Add built-in bench, tall coat storage, shoe drawers, cleaning storage, and better separation between entry clutter and laundry machines.

Builder/interior critique: High practical value. It uses a bright but low-linger zone for the job it is already doing. Keep circulation clear because the space is narrow.

Indicative cost: $8k to $35k.

### 17. Rework laundry/toilet into a better powder-room arrangement

Improve guest toilet access and privacy by making the toilet feel less like a room hidden behind laundry clutter.

Builder/interior critique: Strong family/guest benefit. Plumbing may stay close, which helps cost. Needs careful layout because the laundry/toilet stack is narrow.

Indicative cost: $25k to $70k.

### 18. Add a second WC by reworking bathroom/laundry/toilet together

Create one family bathroom plus a separate powder/WC or improve the existing toilet while preserving laundry function.

Builder/interior critique: One of the most practical improvements for three people plus guests. It is not glamorous, but it solves a real bottleneck. Cost and disruption are moderate to high.

Indicative cost: $45k to $110k.

### 19. Combine bathroom and toilet into a larger bathroom

Remove the separate toilet arrangement and create one better bathroom with WC inside.

### 20. Add a small ensuite to the master bedroom by stealing from office/wardrobe

Use part of the office/wardrobe zone to create a compact ensuite for adults.

## Ranking Method

I scored the options informally against five criteria:

- Solves a real current-house problem
- Works for two adults plus a teenage girl
- Preserves the recently renovated kitchen unless a clearly superior option justifies touching it
- Improves daily light, movement, entry, or retreat value
- Cost is reasonable for benefit

High-cost options can still be good designs, but they are penalised if a lower-cost option solves most of the same problem.

## Best 5 Concepts

### 1. Entry And Sunroom Reset

Relevant options: 8, 9, 12, 13, 38, 39, 46

This is the best front-of-house concept. It keeps the sunroom because the sunroom is the best daylight asset, but it stops treating it as an awkward leftover annex. The work would create a clear visitor arrival sequence at the sunroom/front side, improve the angled exterior doors and steps, add a canopy or weather cue, improve hardware/lighting/house-number visibility, and fit the sunroom out as a deliberate reading, plant, music, or hobby room.

Inside, the sunroom should use low closed storage rather than open clutter, thermal blinds, better seals, possibly upgraded glazing or secondary glazing, and a small heating/cooling solution if practical. The connection to the lounge should remain visually generous, because the lounge depends on borrowed light, but the lounge also needs thermal protection from the sunroom.

Why it works: It solves the unclear front door problem, uses the house's best light, gives the family a second retreat, and reduces pressure on the lounge.

Builder/interior cautions: Do not over-invest in the sunroom until its structure, glazing, weather-tightness, and condensation behaviour are checked. If the envelope is poor, the work should be kept reversible or redirected toward a future rebuild.

Ballpark cost: $25k to $75k for a serious upgrade; $90k to $220k if it becomes a rebuild.

### 2. Covered Deck Outdoor Room

Relevant options: 27, 28, 29, 30, 33, 44, 45

This concept turns the deck into a reliable outdoor living and entertaining room while protecting the renovated kitchen. The main change is a roof, louvred pergola, or high-quality fixed pergola over the main sitting/dining zone, with wind-aware side screening, lighting, drainage, and improved steps/circulation to the lawn and garden paths.

The best version would focus on the kitchen/dining door area as an outdoor dining extension, because the indoor kitchen/dining room is long and narrow. A roof or louvre over the right part of the deck makes the dining zone feel larger without moving the kitchen. Side screens or planting should handle wind and privacy, while still allowing light into the kitchen/dining and entrance/laundry side.

Why it works: It solves the deck's main weakness, improves entertaining, gives the family a real place to sit outside, and avoids kitchen rework.

Builder/interior cautions: The roof should not darken the already narrow kitchen/dining strip too much. Check rainwater, posts, wind loads, neighbour/privacy, and consent requirements before choosing fixed roof versus louvre/pergola.

Ballpark cost: $25k to $85k for a useful covered/shaded deck package; $45k to $120k for a more enclosed outdoor room.

### 3. Lounge Calm And Light Package

Relevant options: 1, 2, 3, 7, 34, 35, 36, 37, 49

This concept treats the lounge as an evening/media/sitting room and reduces its role as a cluttered movement junction. The first step is furniture editing: keep the sofa, coffee table, fireplace/TV relationship, and one or two carefully chosen storage/display pieces, then move excess bookcases, hobby storage, and optional seating toward the sunroom, office, or Bedroom 2 storage strategy.

The next layer is built-in or semi-built-in storage around the TV/fireplace, lighter window treatments, brighter finishes, better task/accent lighting, and possibly a glazed internal door at the kitchen/dining opening. A solar tube or skylight could be considered if roof conditions make it straightforward.

Why it works: It improves the room people actually use for relaxing without large construction. It also supports every other option because a calmer lounge makes the whole house feel more resolved.

Builder/interior cautions: Do not block the sunroom route. Do not add heavy dark joinery in a room that already struggles for direct light. If adding doors, use glazed or visually light doors so borrowed light is preserved.

Ballpark cost: $8k to $35k for furniture, lighting, finishes, storage, and possibly internal glazed doors; $40k plus if roof-light work or significant wall changes are added.

### 4. Bedroom 2 Privacy And Teen-Retreat Upgrade

Relevant options: 23, 24, 25, 26

This concept makes Bedroom 2 work better for a teenage girl without requiring a major extension. The ideal version would first test whether the door can be moved or visually separated from the kitchen/dining edge. If moving the door is too invasive, then the lower-cost path is a better acoustic/privacy door, clearer storage, a built-in desk/storage wall, under-bed storage, and good task lighting.

Because Bedroom 2 is 2.75m x 4.73m, it can work well if it is not treated as a room where every wall gets another object. One calm wall, one study zone, and one clear path are more important than adding storage everywhere. The room's weaker afternoon/winter light means the desk and reading area need deliberate artificial light.

Why it works: It directly supports the teenager's sleep, study, privacy, storage, and retreat needs. It also reduces pressure on shared areas if Bedroom 2 becomes genuinely comfortable.

Builder/interior cautions: Moving the door could affect structure, services, wall bracing, and the kitchen/dining circulation edge. Built-ins should be designed only after final bed/desk/storage priorities are clear.

Ballpark cost: $8k to $35k for joinery, lighting, door/acoustic improvements, and layout; $20k to $60k if moving the door; $80k plus if extending the room.

### 5. Laundry, Mudroom, And Second-WC Practicality Upgrade

Relevant options: 16, 17, 18, 19, 42

This concept tackles the house's least glamorous but most practical bottleneck: entry/laundry/toilet function. The preferred version is not a luxury bathroom project. It is a practical service-zone redesign that makes the laundry behave as a mudroom, improves coat/shoe/bag handling, and either improves the existing toilet access or creates a better separate WC/powder arrangement.

This is especially useful for two adults, a teenager, and guests because the current one-toilet-through-laundry arrangement is awkward. The laundry also gets useful daylight, so it can become a pleasant back-of-house entry rather than a dumping zone.

Why it works: It improves daily family function, guest comfort, and entry clutter without touching the new kitchen.

Builder/interior cautions: Wet-area work gets expensive quickly. The existing toilet/laundry stack is narrow, so fixture clearances, ventilation, waterproofing, and storage depth must be checked carefully. If a small extension is required, the cost jumps.

Ballpark cost: $25k to $70k for a contained laundry/toilet/mudroom upgrade; $45k to $110k for a second-WC or bigger wet-area rework; $90k to $220k if the service end is extended.

## Concepts I Would Not Prioritise

- Full kitchen/dining remodel: the kitchen is recently renovated and should normally be preserved. I would only revisit it if the final masterplan shows that no other option solves the circulation or dining/deck problem well enough.
- Major second-storey or large extension: outside the brief and likely too expensive relative to the specific problems.
- Demolishing the sunroom without a better replacement: it loses the best daylight asset.
- Moving the teenager into the office: the office is likely too small for a long-term teen bedroom.
- Making the lounge more open: the lounge problem is too many roles and routes, not too much separation.

## Recommended Staged Path

### Stage 1: Low-risk decisions and app testing

Model three or four scenarios in the app before any building work:

1. Current layout cleaned up.
2. Sunroom as deliberate entry/reading room.
3. Covered deck as outdoor dining/living room.
4. Bedroom 2 privacy/storage upgrade.

This stage should include furniture movements, not construction.

Likely spend: $0 to $5k.

### Stage 2: Do the best low-to-mid cost improvements

Do lounge simplification, lighting, curtains/blinds, sunroom organisation, entry cues, and maybe deck shade planning. These are high-confidence and do not conflict with the renovated kitchen.

Likely spend: $10k to $45k.

### Stage 3: Choose one larger project

Pick one major project based on budget and family priority:

- Sunroom upgrade if the front/entry/light problem matters most.
- Deck roof/outdoor room if entertaining and outside living matter most.
- Laundry/WC/mudroom if practical daily function matters most.
- Bedroom 2 privacy upgrade if teen retreat is the highest priority.

Likely spend: $25k to $110k depending on the project.

### Stage 4: Reassess before any extension

Only consider extensions after the sunroom/deck/laundry/bedroom options have been tested. A small extension may still be right later, but several current problems can be improved without one.

## Final Shortlist Ranking

1. Covered Deck Outdoor Room
2. Entry And Sunroom Reset
3. Lounge Calm And Light Package
4. Laundry, Mudroom, And Second-WC Practicality Upgrade
5. Bedroom 2 Privacy And Teen-Retreat Upgrade

The deck and sunroom rank highest because they convert existing underperforming assets into daily lifestyle value. The lounge package is the best immediate interior move. The laundry/WC and Bedroom 2 packages are more practical and personal, and should be chosen based on whether the family feels the bigger pain is shared-house function or teenage privacy.

## Second-Pass Review Of The Shortlist

I reviewed the five shortlisted concepts again against the updated kitchen constraint. The shortlist still holds. The main reason is that all five solve real problems while leaving the recent kitchen renovation mostly intact.

- Covered Deck Outdoor Room: keep. It gives the renovated kitchen/dining space an outdoor extension rather than replacing the kitchen.
- Entry And Sunroom Reset: keep. It solves front-door clarity and daylight use without disturbing kitchen joinery.
- Lounge Calm And Light Package: keep, but avoid widening the kitchen/dining opening unless testing shows the lounge needs more connection. A glazed door or furniture/lighting package is more consistent with the current brief.
- Laundry, Mudroom, And Second-WC Practicality Upgrade: keep. It avoids the kitchen and solves a genuine family bottleneck.
- Bedroom 2 Privacy And Teen-Retreat Upgrade: keep. It may touch the kitchen/dining circulation edge if the door moves, but it should not require changing kitchen cabinets or appliances.

The only kitchen-adjacent ideas worth keeping alive are selective threshold changes: a better dining/deck opening, a better lounge/kitchen-dining door treatment, or a future dining bump-out. A full kitchen remodel should be treated as a last-resort masterplan option, not part of the first serious renovation package.
