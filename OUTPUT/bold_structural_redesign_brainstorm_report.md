# Bold Structural Redesign Brainstorm

Date: 2026-06-04
Project: Current House
Purpose: Generate a smaller set of deeper whole-house redesign concepts that include real room swaps, wet-area relocation, sunroom removal/rebuild, additions, and major changes to where living happens.

## Why This Report Exists

The previous broad option sets were useful for identifying themes, but they did not go far enough into the kind of whole-house moves you are actually considering. The strongest current idea is not a small improvement. It is a chain reaction: move the main lounge to the back/light/deck side, relocate laundry and toilet, sacrifice or repurpose the office as wet/service space, demolish or rebuild the sunroom, and reuse the current lounge as a bedroom/media room.

This report treats that level of change as the correct benchmark.

  - Bedroom 2 photos showing a long narrow room that may work better as a lounge/day room than a teen bedroom;

  - sunroom photos showing major daylight value but thermal/privacy/clutter problems;
  - lounge photos showing a darker, route-heavy room better suited to evening/media or a controlled private use.

## Hard Design Truths

1. The real arrival is from the back/garage/deck side.
2. The sunroom is a daylight asset, but not automatically a good room because comfort and privacy are weak.
3. The laundry/entrance side has too much good daylight for a low-use service role.
4. Bedroom 2 is long and narrow; it could plausibly become a better lounge/day room than bedroom.
5. The current lounge is compromised as the main family room because it is darker, route-heavy, and connected on too many sides.
6. The office is one of the most plausible locations for wet-area relocation, but losing it removes a valuable enclosed quiet room.
7. The renovated kitchen should be preserved unless a high-value masterplan clearly justifies touching it.
8. Any deck roof or cover must be designed for daylight, not just rain.

## Cost And Consent Caveat

These are planning-level design concepts, not construction-ready scopes. Wet-area relocation, structural wall changes, new additions, deck roofs, and sunroom replacement may require designer, builder, plumber/drainlayer, engineer, and council input. The broad cost bands below use current NZ renovation guidance only as a sanity check, not a quote.

Broad bands:

- Medium: roughly NZD 60k to 140k.
- High: roughly NZD 140k to 280k.
- Very high: roughly NZD 280k to 500k+.

## 20 Bold Structural Masterplans

### 1. Back-Side Living Rebuild + Sunroom Bedroom Replacement

This is the strongest version of the idea you described.

Bedroom 2 becomes the main lounge/day room. The wall/relationship between Bedroom 2, entrance, laundry, and toilet is reworked so the new lounge can borrow or absorb the bright back-side area. Laundry and WC move toward the office/bathroom side. The sunroom is demolished and replaced with a properly insulated bedroom. The current lounge becomes a media room, snug, or possibly a guest room. Kitchen stays.

Why it is strong: It moves daily living toward the actual arrival/deck/light side and stops wasting daylight in laundry/toilet. It also stops pretending the current lounge is the ideal living room.

Risks: Expensive, likely disruptive, wet-area relocation, office loss, and the replacement bedroom must be carefully designed so it is not just a colder version of the sunroom problem.

Indicative band: High to very high.

### 2. Back-Side Lounge + Office Wet Core + Current Lounge Media Room

Similar to option 1, but the sunroom is not automatically replaced with a bedroom. Instead, the main move is to create a new back-side lounge from Bedroom 2 plus some/all of laundry/entrance, with the office absorbed into a bathroom/laundry/WC core. Current lounge becomes a darker media room. Sunroom is either lightly retained, removed later, or rebuilt only if budget allows.

Why it is strong: It tests the biggest spatial improvement without forcing the sunroom decision immediately.

Risks: Still sacrifices the office and may leave bedroom count unresolved if Bedroom 2 becomes living.

Indicative band: High.

### 3. Back-Side Lounge + New Bedroom Addition At Sunroom Site

Bedroom 2, entrance, and laundry/toilet are reworked into a new lounge/day room. Laundry/WC move to the office/bathroom side. The sunroom is demolished and replaced with a new code-compliant bedroom of simpler shape, proper insulation, controlled glazing, curtains, and privacy. Current lounge becomes media/guest room.

Why it is strong: It preserves bedroom count while moving living to the better side of the house.

Risks: The new bedroom needs careful privacy from master/front/garden. It may be expensive per square metre because demolition, new envelope, foundations/piles, insulation, windows, and interior finishing all stack up.

Indicative band: Very high.

### 4. Back-Side Lounge + New Bedroom Addition At Deck/Garden Side

Bedroom 2 and the service end become the new lounge/day room. Instead of replacing the sunroom, add a new bedroom as a compact rear/deck/garden-side addition, if site setbacks and circulation allow. Current sunroom is then removed, reduced, or converted to a smaller porch/daylight buffer.

Why it is strong: It may put the new bedroom closer to the actual family/back side rather than rebuilding on the awkward front/sunroom side.

Risks: Could crowd the deck/back entry and reduce outdoor space. Any addition near the deck must not make the kitchen/dining darker.

Indicative band: Very high.

### 5. Sunroom Rebuilt As All-Season Bedroom, Bedroom 2 Becomes Lounge, Laundry Stays Mostly Put

Bedroom 2 becomes a small lounge/teen/media/day room linked to the back entry and deck, but laundry/toilet only get modest changes. Sunroom is rebuilt as a proper bedroom. Current lounge becomes media or second sitting room.

Why it is strong: It avoids the highest-risk wet-area relocation while still improving room allocation.

Risks: It does not fully solve the wasted light in laundry/entrance, and the toilet location remains awkward.

Indicative band: High.

### 6. Bedroom 2 Becomes Dining/Lounge Annex, Current Lounge Becomes Bedroom

The current lounge is enclosed and converted into a bedroom. Bedroom 2 becomes a day lounge/dining annex connected to kitchen/deck/entry. Laundry/toilet may move later. Sunroom is either demolished or retained seasonally.

Why it is interesting: It creates a bedroom without necessarily building a new one.

Why it may fail: The current lounge has too many openings and a public position. Making it a good bedroom may require closing openings and may still feel like sleeping in the middle of the house.

Indicative band: High.

### 7. Current Lounge Becomes Master Suite, Current Master Becomes Bedroom 2, Back Side Becomes Family Room

Current lounge is converted into a larger adult bedroom suite or bedroom/media hybrid. The current master becomes the teen bedroom. Bedroom 2 plus laundry/entry becomes a back-side family room. Wet areas move toward office/bathroom. Sunroom is removed or rebuilt as a smaller daylight room.

Why it is interesting: It uses the current lounge's size for a private suite and gives the family a new back-side living space.

Risks: The current lounge is not naturally private. This creates major circulation and opening-control work.

Indicative band: Very high.

### 8. Office/Bathroom Becomes Full Wet Core, Bright Service End Becomes Day Room

Office is largely sacrificed to create a larger wet core: bathroom, WC, laundry, storage, possibly better ventilation. Existing laundry/toilet/entrance becomes a bright day room, study, or back-entry sitting room. Bedroom 2 stays a bedroom. Lounge becomes media/evening room. Sunroom is either upgraded or removed later.

Why it is strong: It attacks the wet-area and daylight mismatch while keeping bedroom count stable.

Risks: It loses the office and may make the central hallway/wet-core side feel tight. It also may not be bold enough if Bedroom 2 remains too constrained.

Indicative band: High.

### 9. Compact Wet Core In Office, Bedroom 2 Stays, Sunroom Rebuilt As Office/Retreat

The office becomes compact laundry/WC/bathroom expansion. Bedroom 2 stays as the teen bedroom. Sunroom is rebuilt or upgraded as a proper office/retreat to replace the lost office. Current lounge stays media/evening. Deck/back entry improves.

Why it is strong: It solves the office-loss problem by moving the quiet room into the sunroom replacement.

Risks: The sunroom site needs real thermal and glare control to work as an office. It may still be too exposed.

Indicative band: High to very high.

### 10. New Bedroom Pod, Office Becomes Wet Core, Bedroom 2 Becomes Lounge

Build a small new bedroom pod/addition somewhere better suited than the existing sunroom footprint. Bedroom 2 becomes lounge/day room. Office becomes wet core. Current lounge becomes media/guest. Sunroom is removed or reduced.

Why it is strong: It solves the bedroom-count problem without forcing the replacement bedroom into the old sunroom shape.

Risks: Site, setback, cost, foundations, services, and roof connection may make this difficult.

Indicative band: Very high.

### 11. Garage-Side Utility Pod Frees The House

Create a laundry/WC/utility pod near the garage/back approach, either attached or semi-attached. Current laundry/toilet is removed from service use. Bedroom 2 can become lounge/day room or stay bedroom with a new bright sitting/study zone. Sunroom can then be removed or rebuilt separately.

Why it is interesting: It accepts that the back/garage route is the real service route and moves laundry toward it.

Risks: It may be inconvenient in bad weather unless the route is covered. It may need drainage and consent work.

Indicative band: High.

### 12. Covered Back Addition As Family Room, Bedrooms Mostly Stay

Add a new family/day room off the deck/kitchen/back side. Current lounge becomes media room. Bedroom 2 stays bedroom. Laundry/toilet may stay or be compacted. Sunroom is removed or made secondary.

Why it is strong: It gives the house the missing living room without sacrificing Bedroom 2 or office.

Risks: It is an addition, so cost rises quickly. It could reduce deck/garden space and shadow kitchen/dining if poorly placed.

Indicative band: Very high.

### 13. Covered Back Addition As Bedroom, Bedroom 2 Becomes Lounge

Add a new bedroom near the back/deck/garden side. Convert Bedroom 2 into lounge/day room. Keep sunroom as seasonal or remove it. Wet areas are improved but not necessarily fully relocated.

Why it is strong: It moves living to the back side and adds a better bedroom without relying on the sunroom.

Risks: The back side may be better for living than sleeping, so using it for a bedroom could waste the same light/arrival advantage unless the layout is very careful.

Indicative band: Very high.

### 14. Sunroom Demolished, Lounge Extended Forward, Bedroom 2 Stays

Demolish sunroom and use part of that footprint to extend or improve the current lounge with proper insulated glazing, more controlled light, and better relationship to the master/front side. Bedroom 2 remains bedroom. Deck/back entry is improved separately.

Why it is interesting: It preserves the familiar living arrangement while replacing the worst-performing envelope.

Why it may fail: It still keeps living away from the real back arrival/light/service opportunity, so it may not solve the core problem.

Indicative band: High to very high.

### 15. Sunroom Demolished, New Proper Bedroom, Current Lounge Opened To Kitchen/Dining

Replace sunroom with a proper bedroom. Current lounge stays living but is redesigned with better openings, light, and route control to kitchen/dining. Bedroom 2 remains bedroom. Laundry/toilet may be compacted but not fully moved.

Why it is interesting: It increases bedroom quality and keeps living central.

Why it may fail: It does not fix the back-side daylight mismatch enough.

Indicative band: High.

### 16. Sunroom Rebuilt As Master, Current Master Becomes Teen Room, Bedroom 2 Becomes Lounge

Build a proper insulated master bedroom/suite at the sunroom site. Current master becomes teen bedroom. Bedroom 2 becomes lounge/day room connected to back/deck. Office may become wet core or stay office depending on budget.

Why it is interesting: It uses the best daylight side for adults and gives the teen a more private current-master room.

Risks: The sunroom site may be too exposed/noisy/awkward for the master, and the current master may not suit teen needs as well as expected.

Indicative band: Very high.

### 17. Two-Living-Room Family Plan

Bedroom 2 plus some bright service-end space becomes a daytime family room. Current lounge remains an evening media room. Bedrooms stay as much as possible, with sunroom either converted to office/guest room or removed later. Laundry/WC compact or relocate only as much as needed.

Why it is strong: It may solve the lounge problem without insisting every room swap happen at once.

Risks: If bedroom count is not solved, it may not be enough. It also depends on how much of the service end can be released.

Indicative band: Medium-high to high.

### 18. Kitchen-Kept Social Spine Rebuild

Keep the renovated kitchen but rebuild the social spine around it: kitchen/dining opens more deliberately to deck, Bedroom 2/laundry becomes a back lounge, current lounge becomes media/guest, office/bathroom becomes wet core, sunroom removed/replaced by bedroom.

Why it is strong: It is a whole-house reorganisation that protects the kitchen investment.

Risks: This is close to a full architectural renovation. It needs professional design, structure review, and staged cost control.

Indicative band: Very high.

### 19. Garden Room Solves The Missing Room

Build a separate garden room for office/teen retreat/media. Then the internal office can become wet core, current laundry can become bright day room, and Bedroom 2 may stay bedroom or become lounge. Sunroom can be removed without requiring it to become the replacement quiet room.

Why it is interesting: It avoids forcing every function into the existing footprint.

Risks: A separate room may not solve night-time bedroom needs, bathroom access, or weather movement. It also adds a detached asset rather than fixing the house core.

Indicative band: High.

### 20. Full Architectural Reset With New Back Living And New Front Bedroom

This is the maximum version. Bedroom 2/laundry/entrance becomes the new lounge/day room. Office/bathroom becomes the wet core. Sunroom is demolished and replaced with a new bedroom. Current lounge becomes media/guest. Deck is roofed/shaded correctly. Back/garage arrival is redesigned. Kitchen is preserved unless very minor threshold work is needed.

Why it is strong: It solves the actual system: arrival, daylight, lounge, WC, teen room, deck, sunroom, and living hierarchy.

Risks: Cost, disruption, consent, services, and design complexity are all high. This should not proceed without measured concept drawings and builder/plumber input.

Indicative band: Very high.

## Shortlist For Serious Testing

### A. Best Overall Big Idea: Back-Side Living Rebuild + Sunroom Bedroom Replacement

This is closest to your own strong idea, and I think it deserves to be treated as a serious contender. It has the best match between actual arrival, daylight, and living use.

The best version is:

- Bedroom 2 becomes the main lounge/day room.
- Current laundry/toilet/entrance are partially or fully absorbed into that new living/back-entry zone.
- Laundry and WC move to a redesigned office/bathroom/service core.
- Bathroom gains a proper WC if possible.
- Sunroom is demolished and replaced with a well-insulated bedroom.
- Current lounge becomes media room/snug/guest overflow, not the main living room.
- Deck gets a carefully designed cover/shade/wind solution.
- Kitchen stays.

Builder test: Can the office/bathroom side physically take laundry + WC without making the hall/bathroom awful?

### B. Best Less-Risky Structural Idea: Office/Bathroom Wet Core + Bright Service-End Day Room

This keeps Bedroom 2 as a bedroom for now, but moves wet/service functions toward the office/bathroom and turns the laundry/entrance/toilet area into a bright day room or back-entry sitting room.

It solves the light mismatch and toilet/laundry problem without also requiring a new bedroom immediately.

Builder test: Is losing the office acceptable if the current lounge or sunroom can become the quiet/work room?

### C. Best Kitchen-Preserving Architectural Idea: Kitchen-Kept Social Spine Rebuild

This keeps the new kitchen but treats the living/deck/service/Bedroom 2 end as one connected redesign. It could include Bedroom 2 becoming lounge, laundry/WC moving, deck becoming a proper social room, current lounge becoming media, and sunroom becoming new bedroom.

It is probably the cleanest design story, but also one of the most expensive.

Builder test: Can this be staged without half-building the wrong layout first?

### D. Best Value/Staging Idea: Two-Living-Room Family Plan

This creates a new daytime family room at the back side while keeping the current lounge as an evening media room. It may not require immediate sunroom demolition or a full bedroom rebuild.

It is not as pure as option A, but it may be a safer way to test whether back-side living really works before committing to the full rebuild.

Builder test: How much of the laundry/entrance area can be released without moving every pipe?

### E. Best Addition-Led Idea: New Bedroom Pod + Bedroom 2 Lounge

Rather than forcing the new bedroom into the sunroom footprint, build a new compact bedroom pod where the site works best, then turn Bedroom 2 into lounge/day room and move service functions.

This could beat the sunroom replacement idea if the sunroom site is awkward, too exposed, or expensive to rebuild properly.

Builder test: Where can a new bedroom legally and comfortably sit without wrecking deck/garden/light?

## Critique Of Your Anchor Idea

Your idea is strong because it has a coherent chain:

- new living room goes where the real daily life and back arrival already happen;
- current service rooms move out of the good light;
- office becomes the sacrificial space for plumbing/service;
- sunroom stops being a compromised semi-room and becomes a proper bedroom replacement;
- current lounge becomes an evening/media/private overflow room, which fits its weaker light.

The biggest weakness is cost and sequencing. It touches almost every expensive category: demolition, new insulated construction, plumbing/drainage, bathroom/laundry work, possible structural changes, deck/weather protection, flooring, electrical, heating, and finishing. It is not a "small renovation". It is a real architectural project.

The second weakness is the office. Losing it is acceptable only if the replacement work/quiet function is deliberately placed elsewhere. The current lounge as media/snug may not replace the office if someone needs a private work room.

The third weakness is the replacement bedroom. If the new sunroom-site bedroom is poorly detailed, it could inherit the sunroom's problems: too exposed, too hot/cold, poor privacy, awkward relationship to master, and uncertain daylight control. It must be designed as a real bedroom from the start, not as "sunroom but with a bed".

Despite those risks, this is one of the first concepts that genuinely solves the house as a system rather than just improving symptoms.

## What We May Not Have Thought Of Yet

1. A **garage-side utility pod** might solve laundry/WC relocation more cleanly than forcing everything into the office.
2. A **new bedroom somewhere other than the sunroom footprint** may be better than rebuilding the sunroom as the replacement bedroom.
3. The current lounge may be much better as a **media/snug/guest overflow** than as a normal bedroom.
4. Bedroom 2 may work better as a **day lounge plus back-entry living zone** than as a teen bedroom because of its length, location, and connection to the bright service/deck side.
5. If the office becomes wet core, the house needs a **replacement quiet/work room** by design, not by accident.
6. A staged version should probably test **back-side living** before committing to the final bedroom/sunroom rebuild.

## Recommended Next Step

Do not pick one final design yet. The next useful step is to model 3 serious candidates in the app:

1. **Anchor Concept**: Bedroom 2 becomes lounge; laundry/WC move to office/bathroom; sunroom replaced by bedroom; current lounge becomes media/snug.
2. **Wet-Core First Concept**: office/bathroom becomes wet core; current laundry/toilet becomes bright day room; Bedroom 2 stays bedroom; current lounge becomes evening media.
3. **Addition Alternative**: new bedroom pod or back/side addition; Bedroom 2 becomes lounge; sunroom removed or reduced; wet core relocates.

Those three should be compared on:

- living room daylight at breakfast, midday, after school, evening, winter;
- movement from garage/car to entry to kitchen to lounge;
- bedroom privacy and quiet;
- toilet access for three people and guests;
- deck usability and shade;
- amount of new construction;
- whether the kitchen can stay untouched.

If those three are modelled, the family can make a more grounded choice before paying for professional concept drawings.
