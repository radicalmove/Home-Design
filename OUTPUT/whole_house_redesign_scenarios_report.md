# Whole-House Redesign Scenarios Report

Date: 2026-06-04
Project: Current House
Purpose: Create 50 alternative whole-house redesign scenarios, critique them, then shortlist the best 5 complete plans.

## Corrected Brief

The earlier report listed 50 improvement packages. This report corrects that. Each option below is a whole-house scenario: a coordinated future plan that can combine room swaps, blocked openings, new openings, sunroom changes, deck changes, wet-area changes, modest additions, and furniture/layout changes.

The scenarios can range from minor to major, but the shortlist is judged by problem-solving value versus cost. The best options are the ones that solve the major problems without becoming prohibitively expensive.

The kitchen was recently renovated. The working assumption is that kitchen changes are unlikely and should be avoided unless a whole-house option is clearly worth the extra cost and disruption. The shortlist therefore favours plans that keep the kitchen cabinet/appliance/plumbing layout mostly intact.

## Current House Problems The Scenarios Must Address

- Lounge is overloaded: media room, sitting room, route to sunroom, route to kitchen/dining, and route from hallway.
- Lounge relies on borrowed light and small windows, so it is not the strongest daytime living space.
- Sunroom is the best daylight asset but has poor seasonal comfort and an awkward front-door role.
- Deck is valuable but exposed to sun and wind.
- Kitchen/dining is long and narrow. The kitchen is renovated, so the best plans use it rather than replacing it.
- Bedroom 2 works but needs more privacy, storage discipline, and a better teen-retreat feel.
- Bathroom/toilet/laundry arrangement is a bottleneck, especially for two adults, a teenager, and guests.
- Front-door identity is unclear.

## 50 Whole-House Redesign Scenarios

### 1. Balanced Existing-Footprint Masterplan

Keep all room uses broadly as they are, but reset the whole house: sunroom becomes a deliberate front-entry/reading room, lounge is simplified as the evening/media room, deck gets a roof or louvre, laundry becomes mudroom-laundry, Bedroom 2 gets built-in teen storage, and the existing toilet is upgraded for guests.

Critique: This is not dramatic, but it is probably the most sensible baseline. It solves the main lived problems without undoing the kitchen or creating extension risk.

Cost band: Medium.

### 2. Deck-Side Social Heart

Keep bedrooms and wet areas mostly as they are. Make the kitchen/dining plus covered deck the main social zone. Add a roof/louvre over the deck, improve deck steps, add wind screens, use the dining end for indoor-outdoor hosting, simplify the lounge as a quieter TV room, and improve the deck-side entrance as the practical front door.

Critique: Strong because it uses the renovated kitchen and deck rather than fighting them. Main risk is darkening the kitchen/dining if the deck roof is too heavy.

Cost band: Medium to high.

### 3. Sunroom As Front Living Room

Upgrade the sunroom envelope and make it the primary daytime sitting/reading room. Keep the lounge as the evening media room, add better thermal separation between sunroom and lounge, clarify the sunroom door as visitor entry, cover the deck for outdoor dining, and keep bedrooms in place.

Critique: Excellent for light and lifestyle if the sunroom structure is worth upgrading. It could become expensive if the sunroom needs a full rebuild.

Cost band: Medium to high.

### 4. Formal Front Entry Plus Private Lounge

Make the sunroom/front path the obvious visitor entry, with a small foyer zone and storage. Add glazed doors between lounge and kitchen/dining, keep the lounge private and calm, cover the deck for family use, improve laundry/toilet access, and give Bedroom 2 a built-in desk/storage wall.

Critique: Good whole-house balance. It fixes arrival and lounge calm without moving the kitchen. It does not add much new floor area.

Cost band: Medium.

### 5. Back Entry Mudroom House

Accept the deck/entrance side as the real daily entry. Build a stronger covered deck-side entry, convert the entrance/laundry into a proper mudroom-laundry, improve guest toilet access, simplify the sunroom as a seasonal retreat, and keep lounge/kitchen/bedrooms mostly unchanged.

Critique: Very practical for daily family life. Visitor arrival still needs site/path cues so people know where to go.

Cost band: Medium.

### 6. Sunroom Removed, Lounge Reconnected To Front

Demolish the sunroom, restore a simpler front porch/entry, give the lounge more direct front outlook, cover the deck, and invest in lounge light/finishes, Bedroom 2 storage, and laundry/WC improvements.

Critique: This may improve thermal simplicity and front-door clarity, but it sacrifices the brightest flexible space. I would only consider it if the sunroom is structurally poor.

Cost band: Medium to high.

### 7. Sunroom Rebuilt As All-Season Family Room

Remove and rebuild the sunroom as a properly insulated all-season family room, with clear front entry, large glazing, better heating/cooling, and strong connection to lounge. Keep kitchen, bedrooms, and deck mostly intact, but add deck shade and improve laundry/WC.

Critique: Potentially excellent but expensive. It turns the weakest architectural appendage into one of the best rooms.

Cost band: High.

### 8. Lounge Becomes Bedroom, Sunroom/Deck Become Living

Block or heavily reduce the lounge-to-sunroom and lounge-to-dining openings, convert the existing lounge into a bedroom or guest room, upgrade the sunroom as the main sitting room, and make kitchen/dining/deck the shared living spine. Improve front entry and add deck cover.

Critique: This follows the kind of radical scenario you described. It adds bedroom capacity but risks making the main living area too fragmented unless the sunroom is rebuilt to all-season quality.

Cost band: High.

### 9. Bedroom 2 Becomes Lounge, Old Lounge Becomes Bedroom

Convert Bedroom 2 into a compact secondary lounge/teen/media room near kitchen/dining, convert the existing lounge into a bedroom, keep the sunroom as front sitting/entry, and reorganise the private-bedroom zone with the office and master intact.

Critique: Interesting but probably not ideal. Bedroom 2 is narrow and near service spaces, so it may not be a great main lounge. It also displaces the teenager from the better bedroom.

Cost band: Medium to high.

### 10. Office Becomes Teen Retreat, Bedroom 2 Becomes Lounge Extension

Move the teenager into the office, convert Bedroom 2 into a second lounge/study/media room connected to kitchen/dining, improve bathroom/laundry/WC, and keep the main lounge more adult-focused.

Critique: Not recommended. The office is likely too small for a teenager's long-term bedroom needs.

Cost band: Medium.

### 11. Bedroom 2 Door Reorientation Plan

Keep room uses the same, but move or reframe Bedroom 2 access so it feels less like a kitchen-adjacent room. Add built-in teen storage, improve bathroom/WC, make the lounge calmer with glazed doors, and cover the deck.

Critique: Strong because it fixes a specific privacy problem without a large extension. Needs careful feasibility check around walls and services.

Cost band: Medium.

### 12. Second-WC Priority House

Keep kitchen, lounge, sunroom, and bedrooms largely intact. Rework laundry/toilet/bathroom as the main project: create a better guest WC or powder-room, better laundry storage, improved mudroom entry, then add lighter lounge finishes and deck shade.

Critique: This is a practical family-function plan. It is less visually exciting, but it solves a real daily bottleneck.

Cost band: Medium to high.

### 13. Bathroom And Laundry Swap Study

Investigate whether bathroom/laundry/toilet functions can be swapped or rationalised within the service end, while keeping bedrooms and kitchen in place. Add mudroom entry, covered deck, and lounge simplification.

Critique: Potentially useful, but wet-area rearrangement is expensive and may not gain enough space. Only worth pursuing with a detailed fixture plan.

Cost band: High.

### 14. Master Ensuite From Office/Wardrobe

Create a compact master ensuite by taking area from the office/wardrobe zone, keep Bedroom 2 as teen room, keep kitchen and lounge, upgrade sunroom entry, and add covered deck.

Critique: Luxurious for adults but probably too expensive and space-hungry. It may damage the useful office/quiet-room function.

Cost band: High.

### 15. Office As Quiet Adult Retreat

Keep bedrooms as they are, make the office a genuinely calm work/reading room, give the sunroom a front-entry/plant-room role, cover deck, simplify lounge, and improve laundry/WC storage.

Critique: Good low-disruption whole-house plan. It creates three retreat zones: lounge, office, and Bedroom 2.

Cost band: Low to medium.

### 16. Teen Suite Without Extension

Keep Bedroom 2 in place but treat it as a teen suite: custom storage, acoustic/privacy door, better desk wall, improved lighting, and possibly a minor door/threshold change. The rest of the house gets lounge simplification, sunroom entry reset, and deck cover.

Critique: Strong for family fit and relatively contained. It does not solve WC capacity unless paired with a service upgrade.

Cost band: Medium.

### 17. Teen Suite With Small Bedroom Extension

Extend Bedroom 2 modestly outward to create a wider teen room or desk/wardrobe alcove. Keep kitchen intact, improve bathroom/WC, cover deck, and simplify lounge/sunroom.

Critique: Nice result but high cost per square metre. I would only do this if Bedroom 2 becomes the dominant pain point.

Cost band: High.

### 18. New Small Bedroom Addition

Add a modest single-room extension for a guest room, hobby room, or future fourth bedroom, then keep existing bedrooms, make lounge calmer, keep sunroom as entry/retreat, and improve deck/laundry.

Critique: Adds flexibility but may be more floor area than the family actually needs. It does not directly fix lounge/entry unless the rest of the plan is also done.

Cost band: High.

### 19. New Living Addition Off Deck

Add a modest enclosed living/dining addition off the deck side, keep the renovated kitchen as the kitchen wall to the new space, convert existing lounge into a quieter media/guest room, and keep sunroom as entry/reading.

Critique: Strong but expensive. It solves living space and entertaining without changing the kitchen, but likely needs consent, structure, and careful daylight management.

Cost band: Very high.

### 20. Dining Bump-Out Masterplan

Add a small bump-out at the dining/deck side to make dining less narrow, keep kitchen run, cover deck, retain lounge, improve sunroom entry, and upgrade Bedroom 2 storage.

Critique: A targeted extension with good logic, but still expensive. A covered deck may solve enough without enclosing new floor area.

Cost band: High.

### 21. Kitchen Change Exception: New Open Social Core

Accept that the kitchen may be partly altered. Rework kitchen/dining/lounge as one larger social core, shift storage/appliances if needed, make sunroom a front entry/retreat, and cover deck.

Critique: Could be beautiful, but it sacrifices recent kitchen work. It should only stay alive as a long-term exception.

Cost band: Very high.

### 22. Kitchen Kept, Dining Rebalanced

Keep kitchen cabinetry and appliances. Rework dining furniture, add outdoor dining deck cover, improve the dining/lounge threshold, make lounge calmer, and use sunroom as daytime retreat.

Critique: Very practical. It gets many benefits of a kitchen/dining redesign without moving the kitchen.

Cost band: Low to medium.

### 23. Lounge Door Control Plan

Keep all rooms in place but reduce lounge route conflict: glazed door to kitchen/dining opening, improved hallway-to-lounge door or slider, sunroom used as retreat, deck covered, and better entry cues.

Critique: Good if the main problem is the lounge feeling like a thoroughfare. It should be tested in the app because too much door control could make movement annoying.

Cost band: Medium.

### 24. Lounge Open Family Plan

Do the opposite: open lounge more to kitchen/dining and sunroom, make the whole front half a more continuous family zone, cover the deck, and use office/Bedroom 2 as quieter retreats.

Critique: I am sceptical. The lounge already has too many roles, so more openness may increase rather than reduce the problem.

Cost band: Medium to high.

### 25. Quiet-Zone Priority Plan

Keep kitchen/dining social, make lounge a quiet/media room, make office a true work/reading room, make Bedroom 2 a teen retreat, and make sunroom a separate daylight retreat rather than overflow storage.

Critique: Strong lifestyle logic with modest construction. It is mostly an interiors, doors, storage, and lighting plan.

Cost band: Low to medium.

### 26. Entertainment Priority Plan

Focus the whole house around entertaining: covered deck outdoor room, dining/deck connection, sunroom overflow seating, lounge as media after-dinner zone, mudroom entry for guests, and improved toilet access.

Critique: Very good for social use. It does not prioritise Bedroom 2 unless a teen-retreat package is added.

Cost band: Medium to high.

### 27. Reading And Music House

Make sunroom the reading/music/daylight room, lounge the media room, office the adult work room, Bedroom 2 a teen study/sleep room, and deck a sheltered coffee/dining area.

Critique: Strong match for the current furniture and lifestyle clues. It is a coherent identity for the house rather than just a renovation list.

Cost band: Low to medium.

### 28. Front Garden Arrival Plan

Rework outside approach so visitors arrive via a clear front path to the sunroom/front doors. Inside, sunroom becomes foyer/reading room, old front door/hallway threshold is cleaned up, lounge becomes calmer, deck remains family entry/outdoor living.

Critique: Good if visitor arrival is a repeated frustration. It is more site/entry design than internal renovation.

Cost band: Medium.

### 29. Deck Entry Becomes Official Front Door

Stop trying to make the sunroom the front door. Make the deck/entrance slider area the official front entry with roof, signage, landing, lighting, storage, and clear route from driveway. Sunroom becomes private family retreat.

Critique: Very practical, especially if people naturally arrive from driveway/deck side. It may feel odd unless exterior wayfinding is strong.

Cost band: Medium.

### 30. Dual Entry Plan

Make both entries make sense: sunroom for visitors/front garden, deck entry for daily family use. Upgrade both thresholds, give each a clear role, simplify lounge, cover deck, and tidy laundry/mudroom.

Critique: Good but could cost more because it improves two entries. It matches the house's existing ambiguity rather than forcing one answer.

Cost band: Medium to high.

### 31. Sunroom Block-Off Plan

Block or reduce the lounge-sunroom opening, make sunroom a separate hobby/entry room, and make lounge a more private internal room. Keep kitchen/dining/deck as the social path and improve Bedroom 2 and laundry.

Critique: Could calm the lounge but risks losing borrowed light. Not preferred unless temperature/noise from sunroom is a major problem.

Cost band: Medium.

### 32. Sunroom Absorbed Into Lounge

Remove the strong separation between sunroom and lounge and rebuild/upgrade the sunroom envelope so both read as one larger living zone. Keep kitchen and bedrooms, cover deck, and upgrade laundry/WC.

Critique: Excellent if done properly, but this is close to a major living-room rebuild. Cost and envelope quality are the main concerns.

Cost band: High to very high.

### 33. Sunroom To Bedroom Conversion

Convert sunroom into a bedroom or guest room, close or control openings to lounge/hall, keep lounge/kitchen/dining as social spaces, improve deck, and add better front entry elsewhere.

Critique: Not ideal. Sunroom geometry/glazing/thermal comfort make it a poor bedroom unless rebuilt. It also removes the best daylight retreat.

Cost band: High.

### 34. Lounge To Master Bedroom, Master To Quiet Room

Move adults into the current lounge as a larger master bedroom, convert current master to office/guest/quiet room, keep Bedroom 2, make sunroom a front sitting/entry space, and keep kitchen/dining/deck as shared living.

Critique: Interesting but likely awkward because the lounge has too many openings and public connections for a good master without major wall work.

Cost band: High.

### 35. Master Plus Sunroom Suite

Connect master bedroom more deliberately to sunroom as a private adult retreat, while keeping lounge as media and kitchen/dining/deck as social. Improve front entry elsewhere so the sunroom is not public.

Critique: This could be lovely but conflicts with the sunroom's likely best role as shared daylight space and front entry.

Cost band: Medium to high.

### 36. Hallway Light And Door Rationalisation Plan

Keep rooms in place but improve the central spine: add hallway light/solar tube, rationalise doors, improve lounge thresholds, improve bathroom sliding door, make sunroom/entry clearer, and upgrade deck.

Critique: Sensible supporting plan but not enough by itself. It should be bundled with another stronger scenario.

Cost band: Medium.

### 37. Service-End Extension Plan

Add a modest extension at laundry/entrance end to create a proper mudroom, better laundry, and better WC/powder room. Keep kitchen, lounge, sunroom, and bedrooms mostly intact; cover the deck.

Critique: Very practical and avoids the renovated kitchen. Cost is the main issue, but it solves entry clutter and toilet function well.

Cost band: High.

### 38. Service-End Refit Without Extension

No extension. Replan laundry, toilet access, mudroom storage, and entrance storage inside the existing footprint. Pair with deck cover, lounge simplification, and Bedroom 2 storage.

Critique: Probably better value than extending if fixture clearances work. It needs a precise layout study.

Cost band: Medium.

### 39. Wet-Area Luxury Plan

Major bathroom/laundry/toilet rebuild, possibly second WC, better family bathroom, better laundry, and upgraded finishes. Keep living spaces mostly as they are except for deck/sunroom improvements.

Critique: Improves daily comfort but may absorb budget without fixing lounge/entry/deck issues. Best only if wet areas are the family's top frustration.

Cost band: High.

### 40. Low-Budget Whole-House Reset

No structural work. Reassign furniture, lighting, curtains, storage, entry cues, deck shade sails or basic pergola, sunroom declutter, Bedroom 2 storage, and laundry organisation.

Critique: Best first experiment. It will not solve everything, but it reveals which expensive changes are actually necessary.

Cost band: Low.

### 41. Medium-Budget Whole-House Reset

Combine lounge built-ins, sunroom thermal blinds/seals, covered or semi-covered deck, mudroom joinery, Bedroom 2 joinery, and toilet/laundry refresh, with no major extension.

Critique: A very realistic renovation package. It spreads money across the real pain points rather than over-investing in one room.

Cost band: Medium.

### 42. High-Budget No-Kitchen Masterplan

Keep the kitchen but do major works elsewhere: rebuild sunroom, cover deck, rework laundry/WC, add Bedroom 2 privacy changes, and upgrade lounge doors/storage/light.

Critique: Strong if budget allows. It avoids wasting the new kitchen but still substantially changes the house.

Cost band: High to very high.

### 43. Full Social Core Rebuild

Rework kitchen/dining/lounge/sunroom/deck together as one new social core, potentially changing the kitchen despite recent work, while also improving Bedroom 2 and wet areas.

Critique: Architecturally strong but financially hard to justify now. This is a dream-plan option, not the pragmatic recommendation.

Cost band: Very high.

### 44. Future Fourth Bedroom Plan

Create a true fourth bedroom by converting lounge or adding a small room, then move living emphasis to kitchen/dining/deck/sunroom. Improve bathroom/WC capacity to support more people.

Critique: Useful only if future resale, guests, or family needs require another bedroom. Otherwise it sacrifices living quality.

Cost band: High.

### 45. Guest-Ready House

Keep three bedrooms but make the office a high-quality guest/office room, improve WC access, create covered deck entertaining, make sunroom visitor entry, and calm the lounge.

Critique: Good if hosting guests matters. It adds flexibility without the cost of a fourth bedroom extension.

Cost band: Medium.

### 46. Garden-Integrated House

Keep internal layout mostly intact, but redesign deck, steps, path, garden sitting area, shade planting, and sunroom/front entry so the house uses the section better. Pair with small lounge and Bedroom 2 interior upgrades.

Critique: Good lifestyle value if outdoor living is important. It does less for bathroom/WC and interior privacy.

Cost band: Medium.

### 47. Privacy-First House

Prioritise quiet: Bedroom 2 privacy upgrade, office acoustic/door upgrade, lounge doors, sunroom separation, and better door/threshold control throughout. Deck and entry get secondary upgrades.

Critique: Good if noise and retreat matter more than entertaining. It may feel more compartmentalised.

Cost band: Medium.

### 48. Light-First House

Prioritise daylight: sunroom upgrade, internal borrowed-light glazing, lighter lounge finishes, skylight/solar tube to hallway or lounge, deck shade designed not to darken interiors, and Bedroom 2 task-lighting/storage.

Critique: Strong response to the daylight mismatch. Needs careful thermal thinking so "more light" does not mean more overheating.

Cost band: Medium to high.

### 49. Builder's Practical Package

A builder-led, low-risk package: no room swaps, no kitchen changes, no big extension. Do deck cover/steps, laundry/WC/mudroom joinery, lounge built-ins/doors, sunroom seals/blinds/entry, Bedroom 2 storage, and hallway lighting.

Critique: Very practical. Less dramatic than a room-swap plan but likely to produce the best value per dollar.

Cost band: Medium.

### 50. Architect's Transformational Package

Architect-led rethinking: rebuild sunroom as living/entry, create stronger indoor-outdoor social core, possibly alter kitchen/dining thresholds, rework wet areas, and test a small addition for dining or service-end function.

Critique: This may create the best final house, but it has the highest design, consent, and construction risk. It is worth exploring only after the lower-disruption scenarios are tested.

Cost band: Very high.

## Shortlist Method

The best scenarios are not the most dramatic. I ranked for:

- Solves the lounge, entry, deck, light, Bedroom 2, and WC problems as a coherent house plan.
- Works for two adults and a teenage girl.
- Avoids changing the recent kitchen unless the payoff is very strong.
- Keeps cost in a realistic band and solves major problems without becoming prohibitively expensive.
- Can be staged without painting the family into a corner.

## Best 5 Whole-House Redesigns

### 1. Balanced Existing-Footprint Masterplan

Source scenario: 1, with pieces from 4, 16, 38, 41, and 49.

This is the plan I would put first because it solves the most problems with the least architectural risk. The house keeps its current room purposes: master bedroom remains master, Bedroom 2 remains the teenager's room, office remains the quiet/work room, lounge remains the main media/sitting room, kitchen/dining stays as renovated, and sunroom stays as a daylight/front-entry room.

The changes are coordinated across the whole house. The sunroom is reset as a proper front arrival and light-rich reading/hobby space, not overflow storage. The lounge is simplified with better storage, lighter finishes, and possibly glazed doors to the kitchen/dining opening so it feels calmer. The deck gets a real shade/roof solution and better steps. The laundry/entrance becomes a mudroom-laundry with better coat/shoe/bag storage and a less awkward toilet presentation. Bedroom 2 gets custom storage, a desk/lighting plan, and better privacy.

Why it works: It keeps the renovated kitchen, avoids risky room swaps, and directly addresses all the existing pain points. It is also the easiest plan to stage.

Risks: It may feel less exciting because it does not create a completely new floor plan. It depends on good design detail rather than a dramatic "before and after".

Ballpark cost: $45k to $140k depending on deck roof, laundry/WC scope, and sunroom thermal work.

### 2. Deck-Side Social Heart

Source scenario: 2, with pieces from 22, 26, 29, 30, and 44.

This plan makes the kitchen/dining/deck side the true social heart of the home while leaving the new kitchen mostly alone. The deck becomes a covered outdoor room for dining and sitting. The dining area is kept visually light and flexible. The lounge becomes a quieter evening/media room, not the main entertaining space. The deck-side entry is upgraded so family arrival is practical and guests can be guided there if that becomes the official entry.

The sunroom still matters, but its role changes: it becomes a morning/daylight retreat, plant room, reading area, or overflow sitting space rather than the only place with good light. Bedroom 2 gets a teen-retreat upgrade, and the laundry/toilet zone gets at least a modest mudroom/WC improvement.

Why it works: It uses the strongest existing asset after the kitchen: the deck relationship. It creates a much better entertaining path and avoids spending money undoing kitchen cabinetry.

Risks: A badly designed deck roof could darken the kitchen/dining and entrance/laundry side. Wind protection must be designed properly, not improvised with an umbrella.

Ballpark cost: $55k to $160k depending on roof/louvre, screens, entry work, and service-zone scope.

### 3. Sunroom As Front Living Room And Entry

Source scenario: 3, with pieces from 7, 12, 28, 30, and 48.

This plan puts the best light in the house to work. The sunroom is upgraded from a seasonal annex into a deliberate front living/reading room and visitor arrival space. The angled front doors, steps, path, lighting, and entry cues are improved so the house has a clearer welcome point. The lounge remains the evening/media room, but it benefits from better sunroom organisation and improved thermal separation.

The kitchen/dining stays mostly as it is, with the deck still improved for outdoor sitting/dining. Bedroom 2 receives storage/privacy upgrades, and the laundry/toilet is improved if budget allows.

Why it works: It addresses the sunroom's contradiction: it is both the best daylight asset and one of the most problematic spaces. If it becomes comfortable, it relieves pressure on the lounge and gives the family another genuine room.

Risks: The sunroom may need more work than expected. If glazing, weather-tightness, or insulation are poor, the cost can jump from upgrade to rebuild.

Ballpark cost: $55k to $180k. If the sunroom is rebuilt rather than upgraded, allow $120k to $260k.

### 4. Service-End Practicality And Teen Privacy Plan

Source scenario: 11, with pieces from 12, 16, 17, 18, 38, and 47.

This plan is less glamorous but very strong for daily living. Bedroom 2 is made more private and functional for a teenage girl, ideally by improving or reorienting the door relationship so the room feels less exposed to kitchen/dining activity. The room gets built-in storage, a defined study wall, task lighting, and better acoustic/privacy treatment.

At the other end, laundry/toilet/entrance is redesigned as a proper service zone: mudroom storage, clearer laundry layout, better guest WC presentation, and possibly a second WC or improved powder-room arrangement. Lounge, sunroom, and deck receive lighter-touch improvements rather than major rebuilds.

Why it works: It directly improves family function: privacy, toilet access, entry clutter, laundry, and teen retreat. It also protects the kitchen.

Risks: Moving Bedroom 2 access or reworking wet areas can become costly. This plan needs precise fixture and door studies before committing.

Ballpark cost: $60k to $170k depending on whether Bedroom 2 door moves and whether the WC/laundry work stays within existing plumbing.

### 5. Medium-Budget Whole-House Reset

Source scenario: 41, with pieces from 1, 4, 16, 22, 25, 27, 38, and 49.

This is the best value-focused version of the masterplan. It keeps the kitchen layout and avoids a large extension, but still touches the whole house in a coordinated way: lounge built-ins/doors/lighting, sunroom blinds/seals/entry reset, deck shade or partial roof, mudroom-laundry joinery, improved toilet presentation if feasible, Bedroom 2 storage/privacy, and hallway lighting.

It is less ambitious than rebuilding the sunroom or extending the service end, but it deliberately solves the major problems at the level where the cost is still plausibly manageable. It also leaves the door open for a later bigger project if needed.

Why it works: It is the strongest problem-solving-to-cost ratio. It improves entry, deck, lounge, sunroom, Bedroom 2, and laundry function without committing to a major rebuild.

Risks: It requires discipline. If every trade package grows in scope, it could quietly become as expensive as a major renovation without the same transformational result.

Ballpark cost: $60k to $150k depending on deck roof scope, joinery quality, and whether any wet-area plumbing moves.

## Options I Would Not Shortlist

- Full social-core kitchen rebuild: potentially good design, but hard to justify soon after a kitchen renovation and unlikely to be the best cost-to-benefit answer.
- Moving teenager to office: likely reduces Bedroom 2 quality without enough gain elsewhere.
- Demolishing the sunroom without replacement: removes the best daylight asset.
- Converting sunroom to bedroom: thermal/glazing/privacy problems make it a poor bedroom unless rebuilt.
- Opening the lounge even more: the lounge already has too many roles and routes.

## Recommended Next Step

The best next step is to turn the top 5 into actual app scenarios. Each should have a 2D plan, furniture layout, key wall/opening changes, rough cost tags, and a before/after movement and daylight comparison.

The first scenario to model should be the Balanced Existing-Footprint Masterplan. It is the benchmark. More radical options should only beat it if they clearly improve daily life enough to justify their cost and disruption.
