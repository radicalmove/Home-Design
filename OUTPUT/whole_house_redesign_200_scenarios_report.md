# 200 Whole-House Redesign Scenarios Report

Date: 2026-06-04
Project: Current House
Purpose: Generate 200 complete whole-house future layout scenarios, then shortlist and refine the best 10 based on solving the major problems without becoming prohibitively expensive.

## Working Brief

Each option is a whole-house scenario, not a single improvement. A scenario may combine room swaps, wall removals, blocked openings, new openings, door changes, sunroom changes, deck changes, modest additions, wet-area rework, furniture strategy, and entry/landscape changes.

The scenarios range from minor to major. The ranking is not "most dramatic wins". The best designs are the ones that solve the major problems with the strongest cost-to-benefit ratio.

The kitchen was recently renovated. Kitchen changes are possible but unlikely, so most shortlisted designs preserve the current kitchen cabinet, appliance, plumbing, and finish layout unless a larger masterplan genuinely justifies touching it.

## Evidence Re-Sweep

Reviewed before drafting:

- `DATA/house_model.json`: room dimensions, openings, daylight matrix, site/deck/sunroom notes, current measured constraints.
- Current saved furniture layout: 53 placed fixed/moveable objects including lounge sofa/fireplace/TV, dining table, kitchen cabinetry, Bedroom 2 furniture, office furniture, laundry appliances, bathroom fixtures, bookcases, piano, and storage.
- Existing design review: recurring issues are lounge route pressure, borrowed lounge light, awkward front entry, sunroom seasonal instability, exposed deck, one-toilet bottleneck, Bedroom 2 privacy/storage, and useful light landing in service spaces.
- Photo evidence: lounge media/fireplace wall, sunroom glazing and play/hobby clutter, deck exposure, kitchen/dining narrowness, entrance/laundry light and storage pressure, Bedroom 2 long/narrow teen use, bathroom/toilet/laundry limitations.
- Current NZ cost/consent context: MBIE exempt-work guidance, 2025 deck cost ranges, 2026 bathroom/full-renovation cost guides. These support broad cost bands only.

## Major Problems To Solve

1. The lounge is a sitting room and a movement junction.
2. The best light is not always where the family lingers.
3. The sunroom is valuable but thermally and spatially unresolved.
4. The deck is useful but exposed to sun and wind.
5. The entry/front-door story is unclear.
6. Bedroom 2 needs to work as a teenage sleep/study/retreat room.
7. Laundry/toilet/entry is practical but awkward.
8. The kitchen/dining strip is narrow, and the renovated kitchen should normally be preserved.

## Cost Tiers

- Low: mostly furniture, paint, lighting, curtains, storage, small handyperson/trade work.
- Low-medium: joinery, doors, minor framing, simple deck shade, entry improvements.
- Medium: covered deck/pergola, significant joinery, mudroom refit, light wet-area work.
- Medium-high: larger deck roof/louvre, bathroom/laundry/WC rework, sunroom thermal work.
- High: structural wall work, small extension, sunroom rebuild, major wet-area replanning.
- Very high: larger extension or full social-core rebuild.

## Builder/Home-Improvement Review Method

I filtered the 200 options through five practical questions:

1. Does it solve more than one major problem at once?
2. Does it improve daily life for two adults and a teenage girl?
3. Does it avoid unnecessary kitchen rework?
4. Can it be staged without wasting early money?
5. Is the expected cost proportionate to the improvement?

The best options are not the wildest. They are the plans that improve entry, lounge, sunroom, deck, Bedroom 2, and service function without turning into a full rebuild.

## Top 10 Refined Whole-House Designs

### 1. Maximum-Impact Medium-Budget Plan

Best source scenarios: 188, 001, 010, 041.

What changes: This is the most disciplined whole-house package. Keep all room purposes and the renovated kitchen. Add a proper but not extravagant deck shade/roof solution, improve the sunroom as a clear front entry and daylight retreat, add lounge storage/lighting and possibly glazed doors, add Bedroom 2 built-in storage/task lighting/privacy improvements, and refit the entrance/laundry with mudroom storage and better toilet presentation.

Why it makes the top 10: It attacks every major problem without a large extension or kitchen rebuild.

Builder refinement: Define a hard scope before getting quotes. The danger is letting "medium" become "major" through premium louvres, custom joinery everywhere, and wet-area scope creep.

Ballpark: $70k to $160k.

### 2. Covered Deck Social Heart

Best source scenarios: 061, 062, 080, 161, 193.

What changes: The deck becomes the social expansion of the renovated kitchen/dining room. Add roof/louvre/partial roof, wind protection, lighting, power, better steps, and a clear relationship to dining. Keep lounge as evening/media room. Sunroom becomes daylight retreat/front-entry support. Service end and Bedroom 2 get targeted upgrades.

Why it makes the top 10: It solves entertaining, deck usability, kitchen/dining narrowness, and some lounge pressure while avoiding kitchen rework.

Builder refinement: Prioritise shade and wind control over size. A smaller well-designed covered zone is better than a large expensive roof that darkens the kitchen.

Ballpark: $55k to $170k.

### 3. Sunroom Entry And Daylight Retreat

Best source scenarios: 041, 042, 045, 057, 194.

What changes: The sunroom becomes a deliberate front-door/reading/hobby room. Upgrade steps, door hardware, lighting, path cues, storage, thermal blinds/seals, ventilation, and possibly glazing. Lounge becomes calmer and benefits from controlled borrowed light. Deck and service end get lighter staged upgrades.

Why it makes the top 10: It converts the best daylight space from a problem into an asset and clarifies visitor arrival.

Builder refinement: Inspect sunroom weather-tightness and frame condition first. If the shell is poor, do not spend heavily on interior finishes until rebuild versus repair is decided.

Ballpark: $35k to $120k for upgrade; $120k to $260k if rebuilt.

### 4. Service-End Refit Plus Teen Privacy

Best source scenarios: 028, 038, 090, 101, 118.

What changes: Bedroom 2 gets privacy, storage, and study improvements; if feasible, its door is reoriented or made less kitchen-exposed. Laundry/entrance/toilet becomes a practical mudroom-laundry-powder zone. Lounge, sunroom, and deck get modest improvements rather than major rebuilds.

Why it makes the top 10: It solves two highly practical problems: teen privacy and family/guest WC/entry function.

Builder refinement: Do a measured fixture/door plan before committing. This option fails if the laundry/WC layout becomes cramped or if moving Bedroom 2 access creates worse circulation.

Ballpark: $60k to $175k.

### 5. Existing-Footprint Practical Reset

Best source scenarios: 001, 003, 009, 187, 196.

What changes: No extensions and no room swaps. Use a staged whole-house reset: furniture and lighting first, then deck shade, sunroom entry/thermal work, lounge storage/doors, mudroom joinery, Bedroom 2 joinery, and toilet/laundry refresh.

Why it makes the top 10: It is the best low-risk benchmark. More radical options should beat this before they are worth pursuing.

Builder refinement: Start with app scenarios and temporary/furniture changes. If the family feels better after layout and lighting changes, defer structural works.

Ballpark: $25k to $120k depending on how far Stage 2 goes.

### 6. Dual Entry Clarity Plan

Best source scenarios: 083, 094, 095, 096.

What changes: Stop treating the two entries as accidental. Make sunroom the attractive visitor/front entry and deck-side entrance the daily family/mudroom entry. Both get lighting, path cues, storage, and clear roles. Lounge is calmed, deck is shaded, and Bedroom 2/service end are improved.

Why it makes the top 10: It directly addresses one of the house's oddest design problems without requiring a new room.

Builder refinement: Keep the two entries visually different. Visitor entry should feel welcoming; daily entry should handle coats, shoes, school/work bags, and laundry reality.

Ballpark: $35k to $130k.

### 7. Quiet Household Masterplan

Best source scenarios: 025, 027, 181, 183.

What changes: Give each family member and activity a clear retreat: Bedroom 2 as teen sleep/study retreat, office as adult work/quiet room, lounge as media room, sunroom as reading/daylight room, deck as social/outdoor room. Use storage, lighting, door control, and modest thermal improvements.

Why it makes the top 10: It suits a household with adults, a teenager, work/study needs, and shared living pressure.

Builder refinement: Avoid over-partitioning. The goal is calm zoning, not a house full of closed-off compartments.

Ballpark: $30k to $110k.

### 8. Kitchen-Kept Outdoor Dining Plan

Best source scenarios: 022, 062, 153, 166, 168.

What changes: Do not change the kitchen. Rebalance dining furniture, make the covered deck the dining overflow, improve deck-side door/shade if budget allows, keep lounge as media, sunroom as daylight retreat, and service/Bedroom 2 upgrades modest.

Why it makes the top 10: It is one of the best answers to "kitchen/dining is narrow but kitchen was recently renovated".

Builder refinement: Before spending on door/window changes, test table size, chair clearance, and outdoor dining furniture in the app.

Ballpark: $20k to $95k.

### 9. Laundry-WC Existing-Footprint Refit

Best source scenarios: 091, 101, 102, 110, 111.

What changes: Keep living and bedrooms mostly stable but make the service end work properly: stack/relocate appliances if useful, add concealed laundry joinery, improve toilet access/privacy, add mudroom storage, and improve deck-side entry. Pair with low-cost lounge/sunroom/Bedroom 2 improvements.

Why it makes the top 10: It gives strong daily-life benefit without touching the kitchen or needing a full extension.

Builder refinement: It needs precise dimensions. Small service rooms can look good on paper and fail in door swings, kneespace, ventilation, and appliance clearances.

Ballpark: $35k to $115k.

### 10. Two-Stage Deck Then Service Plan

Best source scenarios: 193, 196, 197.

What changes: Stage 1: solve deck usability, lounge calm, sunroom role, and low-cost Bedroom 2 changes. Stage 2: rework laundry/WC/mudroom once the family has lived with Stage 1. Kitchen is preserved throughout.

Why it makes the top 10: It is a sensible process design. It avoids spending heavily on everything at once and lets the first changes prove what still hurts.

Builder refinement: Stage 1 must not block Stage 2. For example, deck entry and mudroom ideas should be designed together even if built separately.

Ballpark: Stage 1 $35k to $95k; Stage 2 $35k to $110k.

## Best Overall Recommendation

The best starting point is the Maximum-Impact Medium-Budget Plan, but implemented with the discipline of the Existing-Footprint Practical Reset. In plain terms:

- Preserve the renovated kitchen.
- Make the deck genuinely usable.
- Make the sunroom a clear entry/daylight retreat.
- Calm the lounge and reduce clutter/route pressure.
- Make Bedroom 2 work as a proper teen retreat.
- Improve the laundry/toilet/entry function without overbuilding.
- Avoid any large extension until those moves have been tested.

This is the best balance of solving the real problems and not becoming prohibitively expensive.
